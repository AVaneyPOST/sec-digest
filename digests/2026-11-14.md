## Telecom

**SilentAir — state-level actor inside Asian mobile operators, SS7/Diameter/GTP interception, GTPDoor backdoor** · [CRITICAL]
Primary source is Enea's threat-intelligence team ("SilentAir"); original publication 2026-11-11, major-outlet secondary wave in-window on 2026-11-13. Enea reports that SilentAir compromised signalling nodes *inside* the operator estate at at least five–ten Asian mobile networks (with secondary footprint across "several dozen" countries in Asia / Africa / Latin America) and issued MAP location-update messages to victims' home networks claiming the compromised node now served the target — home networks accepted the update, calls and texts were diverted, and some SMS-based 2FA codes arrived at the attackers moments after interception began. The intrusion uses **GTPDoor**, a backdoor that tunnels command-and-control inside **GTP-C** packets to blend with legitimate roaming / S5 / S8 / S11 signalling. Enea observed and blocked GTPDoor traffic at several protected operators starting Q1 2026. Attribution: Enea rates "state-level, high confidence" and "China-linked, low confidence" (Taiwan targeting weight, no PRC victims, PRC-workday timing); Risky Business characterises it more flatly as a Chinese APT; Infosecurity Magazine floats overlap with Liminal Panda / LightBasin. Enea says infrastructure is "disrupted for now but could return." Operators are unnamed and cleanup is not publicly confirmed. Why this matters for an offensive-security practitioner: this is the first large 2026 signalling-plane intrusion where the compromise sits *on* the carrier network, not just on the external interconnect — any model that treats STP / DRA / signalling firewalls as the perimeter misses this. Immediate hunt guidance: MAP UpdateLocation entries in HLR/HSS logs where the serving-node identifier (SGSN / MME global title) changed to an unexpected GT inside the same PLMN; GTP-C traffic from nodes whose IE patterns don't match deployed SGSN/MME/SGW/PGW stack versions; subscribers whose foreign-service SMS 2FA started failing in Q1–Q3 2026.
[source (SecurityWeek)](https://www.securityweek.com/silentair-hackers-intercept-calls-sms-from-inside-mobile-networks/) · [source (The Hacker News)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/silentair-hijacks-asian-telecom.html) · [source (Infosecurity Magazine)](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/silentair-liminal-panda-telco/) · [source (Risky Business bulletin)](https://risky.biz/risky-bulletin-chinese-apt-uses-ss7-diameter-attacks-to-spy-on-targets/) · [source (CyberWire 15/77)](https://thecyberwire.com/newsletters/daily-briefing/15/77) · Date: Enea report 2026-11-11; in-window secondary wave 2026-11-13

## General high-severity

**CISA KEV catalog 2026.11.13 — +2 entries, both 3-day deadlines (due 2026-11-16)** · [HIGH]
`dateReleased` 2026-11-13T20:24:18.3008Z. Catalog now 1,781 entries (was 1,779). Both new items carry the unusually short 3-day federal-agency due date and `forensicTriage: Yes`, indicating CISA believes active exploitation and wants rapid triage:

- **CVE-2026-114713 — UnrealIRCd server-to-server command injection (CWE-140)**. Affects UnrealIRCd 6.0.0 through 6.2.7; fixed in 6.2.7.1. Lets any user on a network with two or more linked servers inject server-to-server commands executed with server authority. UnrealIRCd maintainer wrote on oss-sec on 2026-11-09: "we are seeing bad guys exploiting this issue on the internet right now." `knownRansomwareCampaignUse: Unknown`.
- **CVE-2026-123039 — Check Point Multiple Products DynamicCLI local-root (CWE-732/862)**. On Gaia OS, the DynamicCLI daemon runs as root and listens on a world-writable local socket without authenticating connecting processes, so an authenticated local user executes commands as root. Affects Firewall, Multi-Domain Security Management Server, Scalable Platforms, Security Gateway, Security Management Server. CVSS ~7.8. Check Point reportedly saw exploitation against "a couple dozen customers."

Neither is telecom-stack core, but Check Point gateways do sit on some telco perimeter paths (operator-IT DMZ, some S-GW / M2M management segments); worth cross-checking operator perimeter inventory for Check Point appliances whose Gaia shell is reachable by any human admin or support-vendor account.

## Worth watching

- **SilentAir attribution convergence.** Enea stays low-confidence on the China link; Risky Business and Infosecurity are already harder on it. Liminal Panda / LightBasin TTP overlap would matter: both have a documented history of telco interior compromise, and a confirmed overlap would make SilentAir the second named operation in the same cluster this year. Watch for Mandiant / CrowdStrike / Lookout follow-ups tying GTPDoor to known Panda/Basin tooling.
- **GTPDoor family.** The GTP-C-tunneled-C2 design makes signatureing hard at interconnect; if Enea or protected operators publish IOCs or GTP-C IE patterns, this is the single most actionable telco-hunt artifact of the quarter.
- **Oracle CSPU full-batch follow-up (2026-11-18).** Comms-stack omissions this cycle (SCP, PCRF, SBC, Session Router, ECE, CCC) will be back at some point; whether any of those shift into an out-of-band disclosure before January is worth monitoring, especially given that SilentAir's technique set would pair naturally with a signalling-firewall or SCP bug.
- **Check Point DynamicCLI exposure sweep.** Local-root only, but it stacks with any unauthenticated-RCE into Gaia (historical or future) to reach a persistent root foothold on the gateway. Expect follow-up research into the DynamicCLI socket surface.
