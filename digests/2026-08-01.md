## Telecom

- **Nanyang Technological University (NTU Singapore) — 84 vulnerabilities in 4G/5G core carrier networks, session hijacking validated on two commercial 5G cores.** News wave breaks 31 Jul (The Hacker News, gbhackers, Reconbee, GuardianMSSP, KSEC). Paper: "Understanding Implicit Trust Errors in Core Carrier Networks through Multi-Agent Flaw Discovery and Analysis." Scope: seven open-source LTE / 5G cores (Open5GS LTE + 5G, OpenAirInterface LTE + 5G, free5GC, SD-Core, eUPF), audited across two control-plane signalling protocols — **GPRS Tunnelling Protocol Control Plane (GTP-C)** and **Packet Forwarding Control Protocol (PFCP)**. Root-cause pattern: implicit trust between core NFs on internal interfaces that were never designed for an adversarial peer — 84 previously unknown flaws, 83 confirmed by vendors, 81 CVE IDs assigned. The headline primitive was then validated against two real-world commercial 5G core networks: **a PFCP Session Modification Request from a network-adjacent attacker causes the UPF to redirect the victim UE's uplink traffic to attacker-controlled endpoints — full user-plane session hijacking on a production 5G core.** First affected commercial vendor: **Dotouch (XproUPF)** — **CVE-2026-8233** (session hijack, CVSS 4.6) fixed, plus **CVE-2026-8232** (DoS on UPF worker loop via libvlib.so `vlib_worker_loop`) fixed. Second commercial vendor unnamed ("a major 5G carrier"), still in remediation. Telecom-offensive read: PFCP is the N4-reference-point protocol between SMF and UPF in 5G SBA (and the equivalent in 4.5G) — it was designed for a trusted intra-core plane and has no built-in mutual authentication in most deployments; any misconfiguration that exposes N4 to a routable or shared-VRF path, or any lateral movement into an SMF/UPF adjacent segment, converts directly into per-subscriber traffic redirection. This is the first public confirmation of a real-world PFCP session-hijack chain against commercial 5G cores. Mitigation: enforce N4 on a dedicated management VRF with IPsec (per 3GPP TS 33.501), rate-limit PFCP Session Modification/Deletion primitives, alert on unexpected `PDR/FAR` overwrites, and audit any tenant boundary that lets non-SMF workloads reach a UPF's PFCP endpoint. Standing gap in commercial-vendor coverage — most closed-source 5G core SKUs (Ericsson, Nokia, Mavenir, Samsung Networks, Cisco Ultra) were not in the paper's audit surface; expect follow-on PSIRT activity. [The Hacker News (primary news wave)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/researchers-report-84-flaws-in-4g-and.html), [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10315), [NVD CVE-2026-8233](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2026-8233), [Tenable CVE-2026-8233](https://www.tenable.com/cve/CVE-2026-8233), [VulDB CVE-2026-8232](https://vuldb.com/vuln/362449), [dbugs.ptsecurity CVE-2026-8233](https://dbugs.ptsecurity.com/vulnerability/PT-2026-39461), [dbugs.ptsecurity CVE-2026-8232](https://dbugs.ptsecurity.com/vulnerability/PT-2026-39460), [CVEFeed CVE-2026-8233](https://cvefeed.io/vuln/detail/CVE-2026-8233), [Reconbee](https://www.reconbee.com/researchers-report-84-flaws-in-4g-and-5g-cores-including-a-session-hijacking-flaw/), [GuardianMSSP](https://www.guardianmssp.com/2026/07/31/researchers-report-84-flaws-in-4g-and-5g-cores-including-a-session-hijacking-flaw/), [KSEC Community aggregation](https://forum.ksec.co.uk/t/researchers-report-84-flaws-in-4g-and-5g-cores-including-a-session-hijacking-flaw/16465).
