## Telecom

- **"Stranger Pings" — House Select Committee on the CCP, released 2026-08-04. This is the closest thing to a telecom-security item in-window.** The bipartisan report from the year-plus investigation into China Telecom, China Mobile and China Unicom lands two findings that matter for offensive telecom practitioners: **(1)** even after Section 214 authorisations were denied/revoked, the three carriers retained "extensive unregulated footprints" in the US — data-centre space, equipment, and network ties — that created routine adjacencies to US carriers' production infrastructure; **(2)** in-the-wild routing telemetry showed **China Mobile International network appearing in routes to Salt Typhoon C2 at least 192 times** while defenders were trying to null-route it, and **nearly 109,000 route-hijack incidents from Chinese/HK-linked networks Jan 2018–May 2025**, with >4,200 involving China Mobile ASNs. Committee is explicit it does not allege China Mobile USA staff knew of Salt Typhoon; the finding is that the residual adjacency itself is the problem. Read it for the operational thesis: enumerate every path from your production estate to *any* residual China-carrier-owned facility (colo cross-connect, IX peering, cloud on-ramp, transit AS route), because that's where the House committee is telling you Salt Typhoon lived. ## General high-severity

- **CISA KEV — three CVEs added 2026-08-04, all with evidence of active exploitation.**
  - **CVE-2026-9198 — IBM Langflow OSS unauthenticated RCE via chained API endpoints.** Affects Langflow 1.0.0–1.10.0. Chain: `POST /api/v1/auto_login` mints a SUPERUSER token to any network caller → `POST /api/v1/validate/code` executes attacker-supplied Python via `exec()`. Full host compromise, unauth. This is the second Langflow KEV in the last quarter — remove Langflow from the open internet, put it behind an authenticating reverse proxy or VPN, and rotate any secrets/API keys/model-provider creds stored in flows on affected hosts. Fix: above 1.10.0. [SentinelOne write-up](https://www.sentinelone.com/vulnerability-database/cve-2026-9198/) · [Langflow GHSA-vwmf-pq79-vjvx](https://github.com/langflow-ai/langflow/security/advisories/GHSA-vwmf-pq79-vjvx). Date: 2026-08-04. CVE-2026-9198. [CRITICAL — KEV, actively exploited]
  - **CVE-2026-18556 — N-able N-central authentication bypass via alternate path or channel (now formally on KEV).** N-able began investigating anomalous activity 2026-07-31; released hotfix 2026-08-02; **the first hotfix failed to close an alternate exploitation path, spawning CVE-2026-18577** (which CISA added to KEV on 2026-08-03 as covered in yesterday's digest). CISA now backfills the parent CVE. Same operational picture: unauthenticated attacker gains admin on the RMM console, then pivots into managed downstream endpoints via Take Control; observed persistence via Cloudflare tunnels masquerading as Cloudflared services, plus rogue `svchost.exe` under user Documents. Patch to 2026.3.1.7 Hotfix 1 or later — treat unpatched N-central as compromised. Cluster-comms confidentiality is gone even when encryption is configured. Affects 11.0.20, 10.1.53, 9.0.116; upgrade to 11.0.21, 10.1.54, or 9.0.117. KEV addition means it's already being exploited against clustered Tomcat deployments — if you use Tomcat clustering, patch today.
